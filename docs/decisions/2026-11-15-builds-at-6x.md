# 2026-11-15: Builds at 6x: a pipeline's runs scroll without laying out the page, and the list reads stored runs

**Asked (Björn, on PR #2479, through the orchestrator):** scrolling a pipeline's runs held 57.8 frames a second at
CPU 6x on a quiet Mac and 44 on a loaded one; Builds' first open in a fresh session waited on the backend's
`pipelines` read, 199 ms at the median. Make both cheaper, hold the scroll in `just frame-budget`, and fix what else
on Builds is over a line.

**How it was measured.** Headless Chrome on the Mac (M2 Pro) over fresh APFS clones of the saved scale database
(`~/Library/Caches/knobas-perf/scale-db/final-home`) through release builds of the windowless backend, the front end
built as the probes build it. The scroll under a Chrome trace (a 4 s script-driven scroll at 3,000 px/s, each layout's
time and dirty objects, paint, the frames' tasks) and with `run.mjs`, both builds alternated; the read with the
backend's own timings and `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)`; opens with `open.mjs`. From about 03:20 the Mac ran on
battery, and Chrome then draws at 30 frames a second whatever the page does: every frame-rate number below was
taken before that, and the time numbers (ms of layout, paint and tasks) are not affected by it.

## 1. The runs' scroll

**Why it was slow.** Not the table. Each move of the runs' window adds and drops a few rows, about 20 dirty
objects, and each such frame's layout took 12 ms at 6x where 4 is what those rows cost. Taking things away one at a
time under the trace found it: Builds' scroller sits in `TfScroller`'s host, a size container (so its body can be
`100cqw`), and Chrome lays out all a size container holds again whenever anything inside it changes. Taking the host's
container away brought a frame's layout from 12.3 to 4.4 ms at 6x; the runs' own container (`.tf-runs`), a block body,
containing the rows or the scroller, and `100cqw` itself each changed nothing measurable.

**Taken.**
- Where the engine keeps a scrollbar gutter (`@supports (scrollbar-gutter: stable)`: Chromium, WebView2, WebKit from
  Safari 18.2), Builds' host is no container. The scroller keeps the bar's 8 px and the clip takes them back
  (`margin-right: -8px`), so the body is the host's width as `100cqw` made it, a band reaches the edge while nothing
  scrolls and ends at the bar while something does. The header is the small container that names the width at which
  *Open in* gives up its words: 1086 px, the host's 1130 less the header's 44 px of padding. An engine with no gutter
  keeps the container, as before. Only Builds: Settings' unnamed queries and the board's lanes read the host's
  container, and their scrolls are inside their lines.
- Each run row is a fixed table of its own in a block body. Not for speed (it measured the same) but because one
  table with a colgroup was wrong: where a container query hid the trigger's column, the columns summed to less than
  the table, and below a 1,060 px runs column every row stopped short of the table's right edge with an empty band. Now
  the branch takes what the others leave at every width.
- A running run's progress bar goes where the duration bar goes (a runs column under 1,060 px): 70 px of bar and the
  word *running* ran over the start time in its 70 px column. Through the design review (keep the established look;
  the chip already says running).
- `just frame-budget` runs `builds-main` and `pipeline-history` by default; their `--real` lines (55 frames a second,
  no task over 60 ms at 4x and 6x) were in `budget.json` but the default list never ran them.

**Not taken.** Containing each row (`contain: layout paint`) halves a scroll's paint (1,040 to 540 ms over the 4 s at
6x) and takes 2 to 3 ms off the 90th-percentile frame, but it snaps each row, which sits at a fraction of a pixel, to a
whole one and moves its text: about 11,000 pixels with large colour steps. Each row as its own container: less layout
and as much more style work.

| layout per frame, median / 90th / max | 12.3 / 14.1 / 16.6 ms | 4.4 / 5.8 / 7.6 ms |
| layout over the scroll | 988 to 1,068 ms | 315 to 379 ms |
| frame tasks, 90th / 99th / max | 24 to 26 / 28 to 32 / 32 to 46 ms | 18 / 20 to 22 / 22 to 23 ms |

**Pixels.** Boxes of every element of the page and screenshots, before and after, at eleven widths, hovered, focused,
scrolled, and on a pipeline page with nothing to scroll: from a 1,180 px window up every box is where it was; the page
with nothing to scroll is pixel for pixel the same; with a bar, 5,800 to 6,200 pixels of the history chart differ by a
colour step of 1 in 255 (its bars' anti-aliasing, from the gutter). Below a 1,060 px runs column the rows now fill the
table and a running run shows no bar, as above.

## 2. Builds' list read (`pipelines`)

**Why it was slow.** Two statements were nearly all of it. The list's 895 runs (91 pipelines, twelve each) each walked
fifteen declared fields out of the run's payload through the declarations, about 85 µs a run: 84 ms. Then the runs'
commits were found by walking every commit's declared sha (5,636), each commit's compressed payload opened again for
every path read: 55 to 67 ms, and 62 ms more once PostgreSQL kept a generic plan for the prepared statement, where
`= any($2)` compares each commit with each of 600 shas one by one. Binding fewer declarations, or resolving each
source's paths once (`first_string_of!`'s shape), saved 10% at most.

**Taken: migration `0088`, `sync.item_run`.** What Builds reads of a run (the fifteen fields) and a commit's declared
sha, one row per item and declaration under the key of the paths it was read with, as `0081` keeps a list's row: two
statement triggers of its own on `sync.item` (no earlier trigger function restated), rows under a key no declaration
gives any more dropped, and a fill at bring-up after the lists' rows (8,256 rows on the scale database). The list, a
pipeline's runs and a repository's Builds tab read a run's row where it has one and walk the payload where it has
none; the commits are found from the asked shas through `item_run_sha_idx`, and only a commit with no row is walked
(fenced with `offset 0`, or the sha test went under the row test and walked them all).

**Same answer.** `tests/it/item_run.rs`: the function carries each macro's expansion; every stored field is the walk's
answer for the reference adapter, TeamCity and Gitea; the list, every pipeline's runs and a repository's tab answer
byte for byte the same with every row, some and none (a mutation reading the branch as the number fails it); the rows
follow inserts, rewrites, stamps, deletes and a changed declaration, and the fill restores them. On the scale
database, the old and new backends answered all 248 Builds reads (`pipelines`, each pipeline's runs twice, 65
repositories' tabs) byte for byte the same.

| `pipelines` in the backend, scale database | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| the command, median of 9 (same minute, both backends) | 185 to 197 ms | 34 ms (66 the first in a session) |
| the list's statement | 84 ms | 14 to 21 ms |
| the commits' statement | 55 to 67 ms (62 more on a generic plan) | 9 to 10 ms |

**Builds' first open** (`open.mjs`, a fresh session, the reads not warmed): at 1x useful at 79 ms, was 124. At 4x
useful at 149 ms (was 153) and drawn at 182; at 6x useful at 220 ms (was 203) and drawn at 286 (was 303), with Chrome at
30 frames a second on battery, so a drawn time carries up to 33 ms of waiting for a frame. At 4x and 6x the open no
longer waits on `pipelines`; what is left is the page's own build of about 800 elements (its longest task 148 ms at 6x)
and the other reads' answers parsed on a slowed main thread. On Windows, at about twice the Mac's time, that is the
first open to watch.
